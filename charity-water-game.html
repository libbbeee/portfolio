A Ripple of Change

An engaging game inspired by charity:water's mission, designed to raise awareness among young audiences about clean water access and environmental restoration.

Back to Projects

Project Overview

Project: A Ripple of Change, a web-clicker prototype

My Roles: Iterative design and development of: gameplay mechanics, resource systems, progression, and UI/UX

Tools: CSS, HTML, JavaScript, Figma

Timeline: 3 weeks

Ripple of Change is a simple, engaging mobile game inspired by charity:water's mission of bringing clean water to communities around the world. Players begin with a polluted lake filled with trash and murky water. By tapping the charity:water jerry can, they generate "Vitality Points" that can be used to clean the environment, restore wildlife, and build community resources such as gardens and wells. The core appeal of the game is watching the environment transform over time. As players invest their points, the water becomes clearer, trash disappears, fish return, and the surrounding community thrives. This visual progression creates a rewarding gameplay loop while reinforcing the message that small actions can create meaningful change.

Design Process

Design Goals

Create an experience that makes charity:water's mission easily approachable and encourages engagement.

Make every action count

Give players a simple, repeatable interaction that generates resources in a menaingful way

Make progress visible

Use visual changes to communicate success. Every restoration milestone should make the world look and feel more alive.

Encourage long term investment

Give players reasons to keep developing their environment through unlockable areas, upgrades, and community structures.

Gameplay Loop

The main gameplay loop connects simple player actions to increasingly meaningful environmental improvements.

  1. Step 1

    Generate Vitality Points

    Tap the charity: water container.

  2. Step 2

    Restore the environment

    Spend points to clean the water and revive the ecosystem.

  3. Step 3

    Collect and recycle trash

    Turn waste into resources for future development.

  4. Step 4

    Unlock and develop

    Build community resources and unlock new areas.

Repeat the cycle to continue growing the community.

Key Systems

  • Vitality PointsThe primary resource earned by interacting with the water container and spent on restoration.
  • Trash collectionA secondary resource system that rewards cleanup and supports construction.
  • Environmental progressionThe world changes visually as players clean and restore different areas.
  • Unlockable areasSmaller sections of the lake provide achievable milestones and a sense of progression.
  • Community developmentStructures such as wells and gardens represent longer-term goals.

Initial Development

Wireframing

Taking my concepts and putting them into a more structured format using Figma. Early mockups explored the placement of counters, the central interaction, and the shop.

Building Core Mechanics

Used HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to begin implementing interactions, score tracking, game state, and progression. I kept the initial scope small enough to make a working prototype within a week.

Iterating on Progression

The Original Approach

My first iteration used one large lake that gradually became cleaner as players clicked the charity:water container. Although the mechanic worked, the area was so large that each action produced very little noticeable change.

The Redesign

I broke the lake into smaller, individually unlockable sections. This created more frequent milestones, made each purchase feel more meaningful, and gave players a clearer sense of how their actions were improving the environment.

Why this mattered

The issue wasn't simply how quickly the player earned points. It was how clearly the game communicated the value of those points. By changing the scale of the restoration areas, I improved the connection between player effort and visible progress. The revised design also created a more gradual introduction to the game's mechanics. This became the most important design lesson of the project: a mechanic can function correctly without feeling satisfying, so feedback and pacing need to be designed alongside functionality.

Final Outcome

Ripple of Change developed from an initial environmental restoration concept into a more structured game prototype built around resource management, visual progression, and community growth. The project strengthened my ability to scope a game, adapt to unfamiliar tools, and rethink a mechanic when the experience did not match the original intention.
